Spotwire Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 August 2026. Applies to the Android application Spotwire (package com.spotwire.app), published by Muhammad Hamid Jamil.

Spotwire sends SMS through a GSM gateway device you or your administrator owns, and can tell the people you choose when you arrive somewhere. This policy says exactly what it stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

What the app collects

DataWhyWhere it is stored
Name and email address To create and identify your account, and to verify your email. Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore).
Your mobile number To verify you own it, to stamp the messages you send, and so people you link with can find your account. Firebase Cloud Firestore.
Messages you send, and the numbers you send them to They are queued for the gateway device to transmit, and shown back to you as history. Firebase Cloud Firestore. Read by the gateway device that sends them.
Nearby WiFi network identifiers (BSSIDs) To recognise a place you saved and tell that you have arrived. Android classifies this as location data, which is why the app asks for the location permission. The identifiers you captured for a saved place are stored with that place in your own account record in Firebase Cloud Firestore. The full list of networks your phone can currently hear is used on the device and is never uploaded. The people you alert are told the place name, never a network identifier.
The position and radius of a saved place Optional. If you give a place a position, Android watches for you crossing into it and wakes the app at that moment, which uses far less battery than searching for WiFi all day. You choose the position, either from your current location or by typing it in. Stored with that place in your own account record, and copied onto the arrival records for that place. The people you alert are told the place name, never a position.
Arrival records: which saved place, at what time, and who was told To show your arrival history and to avoid alerting the same person twice for one visit. Firebase Cloud Firestore.
Names and numbers of people you choose to alert To deliver the arrival message you configured to them. Firebase Cloud Firestore, and the messaging gateway that delivers it.
A WhatsApp gateway key, if you connect one To send arrival messages over WhatsApp instead of SMS. Firebase Cloud Firestore, on your own account record, plus a copy on your phone.

The app contains no advertising, no analytics and no third-party tracking software. It does not read your contacts, your SMS inbox or your call log, and it holds no Android permission that would let it.

Location, in plain terms

Arrival detection works two ways, and both only ever compare against places you saved yourself. It lists which WiFi networks are within range of your phone and looks for the ones you captured at a place. And, if you gave a place a position on the map, it asks Android to watch for you crossing into that circle and to wake the app at that moment, which is why the app asks for all-the-time location. When a place is confirmed that way, the position and radius of the place are recorded on the arrival record.

The app does not track your route, does not record where you are between saved places, and does not build a map of where you have been. Nothing about your location is used for advertising or analytics, and there is no analytics or advertising code in the app at all. While arrival monitoring is switched on this runs in the background, and Android shows a permanent notification whenever the app is actively watching. You turn it on yourself, you are told what it does before each permission is requested, and you can turn it off in the app at any time.

When someone you have linked with asks where you are, the app answers with the label of a saved place if one is in range, and otherwise says the location is unknown. You control who may ask.

Who else sees your data

  • Google Firebase stores the account data and the message queue. See the Firebase privacy documentation.
  • The GSM gateway device reads the messages you queue in order to transmit them over its own SIM card.
  • The WhatsApp messaging gateway, only if you enable WhatsApp delivery, receives the recipient number and the message text.
  • The recipients you choose receive the messages you address to them.

Your data is not sold, rented or shared with anyone else, and it is not used for advertising or profiling.

How long it is kept

Account details, message history and arrival records are kept until you delete them or delete your account. Queued messages are removed from the gateway queue once sent.

Deleting your account and your data

In the app, open Profile and choose Delete my account and data. This permanently erases your profile, your message and arrival history, your saved places (including their WiFi identifiers and any position you set), your links to other people, the record of who you have alerted, the activity log held on your phone, and your WhatsApp settings, and it closes your sign-in. It cannot be undone.

Two things deliberately survive. Messages already delivered to a recipient's phone are not recalled. And a message that was already handed to the gateway device stays in that device's queue as a phone number and a message body until the device has finished with it, because the app is not permitted to reach into the queue the hardware is reading from.

If you no longer have the app installed, email the address below from the address on the account and ask for deletion, and the same erasure will be carried out.

Children

Spotwire is not directed at children under 13, and accounts are not knowingly created for them.

Security

All traffic between the app and its backends uses HTTPS. Account records are readable and writable only by the signed-in owner, enforced by server-side security rules.
